## The curiosity idea (Elon Musk, public statements)

These fed the film's theme only. None of them is quoted, shown, or paraphrased on
screen, and he doesn't appear in the film. The subagent read these from search
results because the proxy blocked the primary pages. Each one below should be
checked against its URL before anyone quotes it.

- "I came to the conclusion that really we should aspire to increase the scope and scale of human consciousness in order to better understand what questions to ask." In Ashlee Vance, *Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future* (2015). Musk is describing what he concluded as a teenager after reading *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*.
- Tweet, 3 Apr 2022: "I am inspired by curiosity. That is what drives me. So let us expand the scope & scale of consciousness so that we may aspire to understand the Universe." https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1510510978286428163
- TED2022 with Chris Anderson (Apr 2022), near-verbatim: he wants to understand what questions to ask about "the answer that is the universe", and to expand consciousness so we can ask better questions.
- xAI's stated goal at launch (July 2023): "understand the true nature of the universe." https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/12/elon-musk-wants-to-build-ai-to-understand-the-true-nature-of-the-universe/
- Tweet, 21 Mar 2021: "...make life multiplanetary & extend the light of consciousness to the stars." https://x.com/elonmusk/status/1373507545315172357
- Starship presentation, Sept 2019, near-verbatim: consciousness appears to be "a very rare and precious thing" and "we should take whatever steps we can to preserve the light of consciousness."
- Ben Shapiro interview, Jan 2024 (reported by Fortune, 25 Jan 2024): "my religion, for the lack of a better word, is one of curiosity, where we want to expand the scope and scale of consciousness on Earth and beyond Earth." https://fortune.com/2024/01/25/elon-musk-spacex-religion-philosophy-outer-space-humanity-population/
- Jordan Peterson interview, Jul 2024: reported as calling it "the religion of curiosity, the religion of greater enlightenment." (Several secondary sources. Not checked against the video.)

## Visual and physical reference facts used

- **Saturn through a small telescope.** At 50 to 100x in a small refractor, Saturn is tiny: a pale cream-gold oval with the rings clearly separate from the ball. The shadow of the ball on the rings may show. It trembles and boils in the air ("seeing"), and it drifts across the field unless you track it. What surprises first-time viewers is that the rings are *real* and that it's so small. So the film draws it tiny in a big black circle, not filling the frame.
- **Aged brass.** Worn brass goes brown-gold with darker patina in the recesses, and it's bright where hands touch it (the focus knob, the grip area). Lacquer flakes. Polished brass is warm yellow with a sharp white highlight.
- **A launch seen from far away.** From tens to hundreds of km it's a slow, bright point climbing on a slight arc, with a thin glowing trail. In twilight the plume can light up. It's silent at first. Sound travels about 1 km every 3 seconds, so any rumble arrives well after the light, faint and low. The film compresses that delay to a few seconds.
- **Parcel sort centres at night.** Long conveyor runs at waist height, roller sections, handheld or ring scanners with a red line, cages, yellow safety rails, high-bay lights. Older sites have sodium lamps, which is why they're orange. Skylights are dirty.
- **Drawing the same character at different ages.** Keep the construction: same eye spacing, same brow shape, same nose, same hairline parting, same identifying mark. Age by adding: softer jaw, lower cheek volume, lid droop, longer earlobes, nasolabial lines, and a thinner upper lip. Hair goes grey at the temples first. Posture: the head moves forward and the shoulders round.
