# After Work: story, beat by beat

## What the telescope means (committed)

Nell's father, Arthur, sorted mail on nights at the old sorting office for
thirty-one years. He worked a year of overtime to buy a second-hand brass
telescope for her eighth birthday and promised to show her Saturn "the first
clear night I'm off." His nights off never lined up with clear skies. The
telescope got knocked over once, which put the dent in the tube and the crack
in the eyepiece, and it went into the hall cupboard. He never got to keep the
promise.

Nell grew up and, without ever deciding to, took almost the same job: nights,
parcels, a time clock. The telescope has been in the cupboard for thirty years.

The film reveals this slowly:

1. Cold open: only the object, in the dark, a thin line of light across it.
2. Warehouse: she hums four notes under the conveyor noise. We don't know the tune yet.
3. The cupboard: she opens it.
4. The case: a pencilled note from her father in the lid.
5. The memory: her father pointing up, then checking his watch and leaving for
   his shift with his lunch tin. Small Nell alone with the telescope.
   The rhyme lands: he traded his nights too.
6. Saturn: she keeps his promise, thirty years late, and then keeps it again,
   for her own kid.
7. The end: she is the one with nights to spare now. She hums his tune, in full,
   for the first time in the film.

The dent stays. She fixes the eyepiece and polishes the brass but never taps
the dent out. It's part of it.

## The two ideas under everything

- **Paycheck vs time.** What she's afraid of losing is a wage. What she gets back
  is time. The film never calls the job a prison: she is fast, precise, proud of
  it, and the machines are shown doing her exact motion, not mocking it.
- **Curiosity as the thing worth organising a life around.** Looking up, asking
  the next question, and light spreading outward so it isn't small or alone.
  Shown only through image: one star through a grimy skylight, a book of
  questions, Saturn in a trembling circle, a thin line climbing into the dark,
  and warm points of light drifting outward from the planet at the end.

## Scene-by-scene

**Cold open.** A hall cupboard in the dark. The door is open a crack, and a
thin blade of light lies across a brass tube. Dust turns in it. The light
catches a crack in an eyepiece. One plucked phrase, four notes, unresolved.
Title: *After Work*.

**2027. The routine.** A parcel warehouse at 3am. Low, cramped camera,
sodium lamps in haze. Nell (49) scans parcels: take, beep, turn, place. She is
quick and exact. She glances up at a grimy skylight with one star in it and
hums four notes nobody hears. The time clock: clunk, 06:02. Her kitchen at
dawn: only the kettle steam moves until she sits down in her coat.

**The machines.** New sorting arms: pale, rounded, each with a small amber
lamp. Quiet. As she passes, one pauses mid-motion to let her by. Match cut to
her hands' exact motion, now done by a gripper, twice as fast. Her gloved hand
enters the frame, hesitates, and withdraws.

**The fear.** A hand-lettered letter on the kitchen table. Her face, still.
From the doorway, at a nine-year-old's height, Sam watches his mum's back.
Rain starts. No music.

**2028. Winter.** The street at dusk in snow, drawn as lines. A shuttered
shop. Her breath fogs. Kitchen: coat on indoors, sums in a notebook, Sam doing
homework in a hat. The community hall, windows warm in the snow; they hesitate
at the door and go in. A noticeboard of hand-lettered cards: bike repairs,
childminding swaps, lifts to the hospital, "learning together, Thursdays."
Later, a second letter at the same table. She reads it and her shoulders drop.
Thaw drips outside.

**2029. The cupboard.** She can't sleep. The same thin line of light, now seen
from the hall. She opens the door. Morning: the telescope on the kitchen table,
a folded note in the case lid in her father's pencil. The memory: dusk, the
back step, her father pointing up, then checking his watch and leaving with
his lunch tin. Small Nell alone with the telescope.

**Learning.** A small warm light on the kitchen table beside an open book. The
pages fill with drawings of the sky, then of lenses, light bending through
glass. The window light changes through days. The plant on the shelf grows. She
fixes the eyepiece. She polishes the brass. She leaves the dent.

**Saturn.** The back step, early spring, cold. Sam (11) on the step in his
coat. She looks. The circle of the eyepiece: blur, drift, and then Saturn slides
in: tiny, pale gold, rings, trembling in the air. Her face. She pulls back and
beckons. Sam looks and laughs out loud.

**2031 onward. Opening up.** Chalk Saturns on the back step. A rooftop at
golden hour, neighbours on folding chairs. The same street across years: the
car park dug into gardens, a workshop door open, benches, bunting, old and
young. The kitchen as a clock: shelves filling, Sam growing, the warm light
still there. The community hall again, full, Nell (58) at the front.

**2047. The stars.** The back step at night. Nell (69) in a chair under a
blanket. Sam (29) and his daughter at the telescope. Crickets. She hums her
father's tune. On the horizon a thin bright line climbs into the dark. She
doesn't use the telescope. She watches with her own eyes, and smiles. The
rumble arrives late. The camera pulls back from the lens, out past the step,
the street, the town, the curve of the planet, to small warm lights drifting
outward among the stars. The tune plays in full. Hold on the stars.

One line: *For everyone who ever worked nights.*
